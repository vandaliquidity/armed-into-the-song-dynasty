# ART_DIRECTION.md - what "Northern Song" must look like here

Setting: **Northern Song, about 1120 CE (Xuanhe era, Emperor Huizong), the north-west frontier** (Qinghe area, facing the Western Xia). A small fortified farming/garrison village that grows into a town. Not the capital Kaifeng, not the rich south: a provincial frontier place. Poor and practical at the start, more tiled and more civic as prosperity rises.

The current Song sheets in `current/` are *not terrible* - the complaint is that they are **flat, single-image, top-down-ish stamps with no inside and no shared style** while the villagers around them are animated and carry goods. The re-render must keep the Song identity and add **a readable interior (cutaway), a removable roof, and one unified hand** across all ~34 building types.

## 1. The ten things that make it read as Song (do these)
| # | Feature | How to draw it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deep, gently curved eaves (出檐深遠)** | Roof overhangs the walls generously. Roof line is a soft, low-pitched curve, not a steep Qing swoop and not an upturned pagoda corner. Corners rise only very slightly. |
| 2 | **Grey clay tile (灰瓦 / 青瓦)** | Charcoal to mid-grey, matte, small tile rows with a plain ridge. Poor houses: thatch or plank. Glazed tile only on the grandest official building and only a muted green-grey; **never bright yellow**. |
| 3 | **Roof types** | 懸山 (overhanging gable, the common one) and 歇山 (hip-and-gable, for halls/yamen/treasury/gate towers). 廡殿 (full hip) only for the town gate tower. **Avoid 硬山 (flush gable): that is a Ming/Qing habit.** |
| 4 | **Chiwen ridge ends (鴟吻/鴟尾)** | Simple fish-tail / owl-tail ornament at the ridge ends of important roofs only; plain ridge elsewhere. No Qing beast rows on the hip ridges. |
| 5 | **Visible bracket sets (斗拱)** | Under the eaves of official buildings, big and simple relative to the post (Song brackets are large). Provincial buildings: a plain bracket block or a bare beam end. No dense Qing mini-bracket rows. |
| 6 | **Raised platform (台基)** | Every serious building stands on a low rammed-earth/brick/stone platform with a few steps. Mark the edge with a stone kerb. |
| 7 | **Walls** | Rammed earth (夯土) and earth-brick lower walls, white lime-plaster panels between brown timber posts. Frontier: more earth, less timber. |
| 8 | **Straight-lattice windows (直欞窗) and plank doors (版門)** | Simple vertical-bar windows; double-leaf plank doors with rows of round iron studs (門釘). Official halls may have lattice doors (格子門). No round "moon" windows, no complex ice-crack lattice. |
| 9 | **Timber: unpainted or dark brown; vermilion only for official buildings** | Vermilion (朱) posts for yamen/treasury/hall; everything else natural wood, oiled brown or weathered grey. |
| 10 | **Song everyday life** | Raised-floor wooden platforms and stone-paved/brick floors; **high furniture is already in use** (stools, benches, chests, tables, folding chairs 交椅); bamboo and woven matting; large ceramic jars; bamboo baskets; shoulder-poles. |

## 2. Things that make it read as wrong (do NOT draw)
| Do not | Why |
|---|---|
| Bright yellow / imperial-gold glazed roofs | Ming/Qing imperial convention |
| Huizhou "horse-head" stepped gable walls (馬頭牆), flush gables 硬山 | Ming/Qing southern style |
| Flying-eave pagoda roofs with steep upturned corners, Qing red palace walls | Qing |
| Dragon/phoenix carved beams in heavy gold, 垂花門, round moon gates | Ming/Qing garden style |
| Tatami, sliding paper shoji, Japanese roof curves, torii | Japan |
| Terracotta pantiles, flat roofs, mud-brick domes, Middle-Eastern tilework | The reference screenshots show these - they are for the *cutaway format only*, not for style |
| Neon-saturated paint, pastel fantasy colours | Wrong mood |
| Modern items: glass panes, nails/screws visibly modern, plastic, wheelbarrows with rubber tyres, gas lamps | Anachronism |
| Maize/corn, potato, chili pepper, tomato, sweet potato, tobacco | New-World crops; not in China c.1120. Use millet (粟), wheat, barley, soybean, hemp, sesame; avoid sorghum (高粱), which is later |
| Cotton cloth as a large industry in the north | Cotton spread widely only later; use hemp (麻), ramie, silk, wool, felt |
| Gunpowder cannon, handguns | Not yet; the game's "fire lance" (火槍) and "thunder-crash bomb" (霹靂炮) are period correct, simple bamboo/iron tubes |
| Chinese characters generated by the image model | Always garbled. Leave boards BLANK and add text with `tools/add_signboard_text.py` |

## 5. Signboards, banners and Chinese text
* Period signs: hanging 招牌 (vertical board hung from the eave, text top to bottom), wine flags 酒旗/酒帘 (cloth strip on a pole, one or two characters), shop-front 招幌 objects (a gourd for a doctor, a bundle of arrows for a bowyer, a wine jar for a winehouse), and gaudy bamboo-and-cloth festival gate frames (彩樓歡門) on the grand wineshops as in the *Qingming Scroll*.
* **Traditional characters** (繁體) as in the `board` column of `buildings.csv`. Keep to 2-3 characters per board.
* Vertical boards read top to bottom. Horizontal plaques: set left to right (the convention used in the game UI).
* Style: black or dark brown ink brush characters on a plain pale or dark wooden board; no neon, no gold lettering.
* The game draws the village name over the gate plaque itself: the three gate sprites have a **blank** plaque.
* **Never trust generated glyphs.** Paint boards blank, then run `tools/add_signboard_text.py`. See `ACCEPTANCE.md`.

## 6. References to search for (cannot be bundled; look these up)
* **清明上河圖** (Zhang Zeduan, c.1100-1125, Palace Museum Beijing): the best single visual source for Northern Song street scenes, shops, signboards, carts, roofs, bridges, wine houses.
* **營造法式** (Yingzao Fashi, Li Jie, 1103): the official building manual: roof types, bracket sets, plinths, windows, doors. Use for correct roof/bracket proportions.
* **Surviving Song and near-Song timber buildings**: 晉祠聖母殿 (Jinci, Taiyuan, 1102), 隆興寺摩尼殿 (Longxing Temple, Zhengding, 1052), 初祖庵大殿 (Dengfeng, Shaolin, 1125). Liao/Jin neighbours of similar style: 奉國寺大殿, 善化寺, 應縣木塔.
* **千里江山圖** (Wang Ximeng, 1113): rural houses, thatch, bridges, water mills.
* **耕織圖** (Lou Shu, c.1145, Southern Song but derived from earlier practice): farm and textile tools and workshops (use for weaver, papermill-like processes, with care).
* **天工開物** is Ming: do NOT use for style, only for process hints, and treat details with suspicion.
* 武經總要 (Wujing Zongyao, 1044): Song military technology (crossbows, siege gear, early fire weapons) for the workshops.
* Excavated Song tomb murals and brick reliefs (Henan, Shanxi: 白沙宋墓, 偃師, 侯馬) show real Song interiors and furniture.

## 7. The three supplied reference images (`refs/`)
They are screenshots from an advertisement of another mobile game (UI trimmed copies in `refs/cropped_no_ui/`) and are **quality bars for the cutaway format, not for style**; do not copy their art. Their view is steeper (nearly top-down) than our 38 degrees, and they contain people (we draw none: the game animates them):
* `ref_01_olive_press_courtyards_cutaway.png`: several courtyard buildings with roofs removed; each room shows its machine, workers and piles.
* `ref_02_dye_and_cloth_workshops_cutaway.png`: dye vats, drying racks, cloth bolts, clearly squarish rooms.
* `ref_03_farmstead_rooms_cutaway.png`: a farmstead cut into rooms with beds, tools, stock.
What to copy: cutaway readability, squarish rooms, crisp props, props that tell the building's job at a glance, a consistent soft light, a low front/right wall so you can see in.
What NOT to copy: terracotta/tatami/Middle-Eastern tile materials, their colours, their roof forms.

## 8. How a cutaway is drawn here
1. Rear wall and left wall are full height (they are *behind* the room, so they do not hide it).
2. Front wall and right wall are cut down to a low kerb/half-wall (about knee height) or open posts.
3. The floor, the workstation and the piles are fully visible; the room is roughly **square in plan** (width : depth between 1:1 and 1.3:1).
4. The roof is a **separate layer** (same canvas) that the game fades out when the player zooms in, so it must be a complete, believable roof when seen alone (rafters visible on the underside is not needed).
5. Workers stand at fixed stations given per building (`stations` in `buildings.csv`): keep those floor spots **empty of props** (about 30 x 24 wu each).
6. Goods piles: input pile at the left, output rack at the right, both inside the room and kept clear; the game draws the live stacks itself, so paint the *empty* pallet/rack.
